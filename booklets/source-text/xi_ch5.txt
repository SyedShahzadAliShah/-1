##P98
5.1 Introduction to Computing
Computing is now an important part of our daily lives. We use computers,
mobile phones, the internet, and smart devices everywhere. In Pakistan, it is
becoming vital in schools, hospitals, banks, farms, and industries.
5.1.1 Artificial Intelligence (AI)
★ GOLDEN TOPIC
AI is called "artificial" because it is created by humans, and "intelligence" means
it can perform tasks that require thinking. AI systems use data to make
decisions.
Example 1: Mobile phone face recognition to unlock screens.
Example 2: YouTube suggesting videos based on your interests.
Example 3: Chatbots answering questions on a website.
AI in Smart Systems
AI often acts as the "brain" of a system, working together with the Internet of Things
(IoT) and Data Analytics. IoT devices gather data, Analytics sorts it, and AI makes the
final decision.
(e.g., If a room is hot, AI decides to turn on the AC automatically based on sensor data).

5.1.2 Internet of Things (IoT)
★ GOLDEN TOPIC
The Internet of Things (IoT) is a system in which physical devices are connected
to the Internet and can collect and share data to perform tasks automatically.
Example: A smartwatch collects heart rate data and sends it to a mobile phone to
display.
Components of IoT Systems
IoT relies on six foundational components to work properly:
Devices that collect information from the physical world. They are the "eyes and ears" of
the system.
Temperature sensor
Motion sensor
Fingerprint sensor
Smoke / Soil moisture sensor
Devices that perform physical actions in response to system commands. They convert
digital signals into physical movement.
Electric Motor / Switch
Automatic door opener
Water valve / Fan controller

##P101
5.1.3 Data Analytics
Data Analytics is the process of collecting, organizing, cleaning, studying, and
presenting data to find useful information. It helps people make better
decisions.
Example: A college collects exam marks, calculates percentages, and identifies which
students need extra help. This entire process is Data Analytics.
Components of Data Analytics
It comprises five key components:
1. Data Collection
Gathering raw information from multiple sources (websites, surveys, sensors, hospital
records, bank transactions).
2. Data Storage
Storing data safely and properly after collection. It can be stored in files, databases,
spreadsheets, or the cloud.
3. Data Cleaning
Identifying and correcting errors in data. Raw data may contain:
Duplication of records
Incorrect values (typos)
Missing files or records
Improper formats

5.2 Uses of IoT in Different Areas
IoT improves efficiency, saves time, and enhances the overall quality of life. It
plays a significant role in developing modern infrastructure.
1. Smart Cities
Traffic Management: Sensors adjust
traffic signals automatically to reduce
congestion.
Street Lighting: Lights turn ON/OFF
based on daylight or movement.
Parking: Sensors guide drivers to empty
parking spaces.
Waste Management: Smart bins send
alerts when full.
2. Industrial Automation
Sensors check machine temperature
and vibration to prevent breakdowns.
Automated machines perform repeated
tasks precisely.
Quality control sensors check products
during manufacturing (e.g., Textile
industries in Sindh).
3. Healthcare (Smart Health)
Smartwatches continuously measure heart rate and vitals.
Remote monitoring devices send patient data directly to doctors (vital for rural areas
in Pakistan).
Emergency alert devices inform hospitals instantly.

5.2.1 AI in Education in Pakistan
Artificial Intelligence is gradually becoming an important part of Pakistan's
education system, improving teaching, learning, and administration.
Practical Uses
Learning Management Systems (LMS).
Automated grading and digital testing.
AI-based career counselling.
Benefits

Identifies students who need extra help.
Supports students with disabilities

Major Challenges in Pakistan

(sensors/circuits), and Problem-Solving.

5.3 Information Sources
★ GOLDEN TOPIC
Information sources are ways to find knowledge and data. While the internet
has vast information, not all of it is accurate. Sources are divided into three main
categories:
Give original, firsthand information straight from people, events, or official records.
They are highly authentic.
Examples: Interviews, photographs, diaries, NADRA official records.
Information that has already been collected and explained by someone else. They
summarize or analyze primary data.
Examples: Textbooks, news reports, research papers, Bureau of Statistics reports.

##P107
Information Sources (Continued)
Provide highly summarized and organized information derived from both primary
and secondary sources. Used for quick reference.
Examples: Encyclopedias, Wikipedia, Dictionaries, Handbooks.
When do sources become unreliable?
In the digital age, students must check facts carefully. A source is unreliable if:
Source Type
Reasons for Unreliability
Primary Sources
Information is incomplete, data is recorded incorrectly, or
false information was deliberately provided.
Secondary Sources
The information is outdated, lacks proper
references/citations, or has not been verified by an expert.
Tertiary Sources
Comes from an unknown/unverified website, or the
information is oversimplified and misses key facts.
👨‍🏫 Teacher's Tip
Teach students the rule of thumb: If an online article does not state the
author's name, the date of publication, or list its references, it should not be
trusted for academic work.

5.5 Assistive Technologies
★ GOLDEN TOPIC
Assistive technologies are devices, software, and systems that help people with
disabilities or functional limitations. They allow more people to participate
equally in school, work, and society.
Categories of Assistive Technologies:
1. Mobility Aids
Help people move from one place to
another.
Examples: Smart wheelchairs, stair lifts.
2. Sensory Aids
Support vision and hearing to improve
interaction.
Examples: Hearing aids, Screen readers
(VoiceOver), Braille displays.
3. Communication Aids
Assist individuals with speech difficulties
to express themselves.
Examples: Speech-generating devices,
Text-to-speech apps.
4. Cognitive / Learning Aids
Help people with memory, reading, and
understanding.
Examples: Audiobooks, spell-checkers,
digital reminder apps.

##P110
Assistive Tech Importance & Careers
5.5.1 Importance of Assistive Technologies
Equal Access to Education: Screen readers allow blind students to read digital
textbooks equally with their peers.
Independence in Daily Life: Mobility aids reduce the need for constant human
assistance.
Social Inclusion: Breaks down barriers, allowing individuals to work, study, and
socialize confidently.
5.5.2 Career Connection
Designing assistive tech is a highly rewarding career path:
Assistive Software Developer: Creates speech-to-text tools.
Biomedical Engineer: Designs artificial limbs and hearing aids.
UX/UI Accessibility Expert: Ensures websites are easy to navigate for visually impaired
users.
Summary
AI: Enables machines to learn, recognize patterns, and make decisions.
IoT Components: Sensors (collect data), Actuators (take action), Connectivity, Data
Processing, Cloud, and UI.
Data Analytics: The pipeline of collecting, storing, cleaning, analyzing, and visualizing data
for insights.
Information Sources: Primary (direct/original), Secondary (analyzed by others), Tertiary
(summarized lists/encyclopedias).
Impacts: Computing accelerates business and education but risks privacy, job loss, and
screen addiction.
Assistive Tech: Tools like screen readers and smart wheelchairs that ensure equality,
independence, and inclusion for people with disabilities.
