[y=54 x=57 B19.2] 6.7 Presenting Data Using Digital Tools
[y=94 x=57 R12.0] Collecting data is only one part of the work. Students must also present their
[y=112 x=57 R12.0] findings so others can understand them quickly and easily. Digital tools make
[y=130 x=57 R12.0] this much easier.
[y=232 x=65 B14.4] 6.7.1 Spreadsheets
[y=261 x=65 R10.8] Spreadsheets such as MS Excel, Google Sheets, and Apple Numbers help students
[y=276 x=65 R10.8] arrange information in rows and columns.
[y=301 x=80 R10.2] Enter data systematically.
[y=316 x=80 R10.2] Calculate totals, averages, and percentages.
[y=332 x=80 R10.2] Sort and filter records.
[y=347 x=80 R10.2] Create charts and graphs automatically.
[y=398 x=65 B14.4] 6.7.2 Presentations
[y=426 x=65 R10.8] Software like PowerPoint, Google Slides, and Canva helps students explain findings to
[y=442 x=65 R10.8] an audience in an organized way.
[y=467 x=80 R10.2] Begin with a clear title and research question.
[y=482 x=80 R10.2] Use short bullet points instead of long paragraphs.
[y=497 x=80 R10.2] Include tables, charts, and key findings.
[y=512 x=80 R10.2] Keep the design simple and readable.
