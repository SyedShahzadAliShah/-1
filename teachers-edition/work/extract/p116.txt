[y=102 x=65 B14.4] 6.4.3 Prototypes
[y=131 x=65 R10.8] A prototype is a simple early version of a product, model, or solution. It allows an idea
[y=147 x=65 R10.8] to be tested before time and effort are spent on the final version. Users try the
[y=163 x=65 R10.8] prototype and give feedback, which becomes useful data for improvement.
[y=246 x=65 B14.4] 6.4.4 Observation
[y=275 x=65 R10.8] In observation, the researcher watches what people do or what happens in a situation
[y=291 x=65 R10.8] and records the results carefully. No direct questions are asked. The data may be
[y=307 x=65 R10.8] qualitative (notes) or quantitative (counts).
[y=391 x=65 B14.4] 6.4.5 Simulations
[y=420 x=65 R10.8] A simulation is a computer-based model of a real situation. Variables can be
[y=435 x=65 R10.8] changed to see what happens. Simulations are highly useful when real experiments
[y=452 x=65 R10.8] are costly, risky, or difficult to perform.
