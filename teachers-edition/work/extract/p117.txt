[y=97 x=68 B10.8] ★ GOLDEN TOPIC
[y=130 x=57 R12.0] Whenever data is used, it is important to know where it came from. The source
[y=148 x=57 R12.0] affects how much control the researcher had over collecting it and how
[y=166 x=57 R12.0] confidently the results can be used.
[y=203 x=69 B14.4] 6.5.1 Primary Data
[y=231 x=69 R10.8] Primary data is collected first-hand
[y=247 x=69 B10.8] by the researcher for a particular
[y=264 x=69 R10.8] purpose. It is gathered directly from
[y=279 x=69 R10.8] people, events, or situations rather
[y=296 x=69 R10.8] than taken from an already published
[y=312 x=69 R10.8] source.
[y=392 x=69 B10.2] Sources: Interviews, surveys,
[y=407 x=69 B10.2] observations, experiments.
[y=203 x=317 B14.4] 6.5.2 Secondary Data
[y=231 x=317 R10.8] Secondary data has already been
[y=247 x=317 B10.8] collected by someone else and is
[y=264 x=317 R10.8] being used again for a new purpose. It
[y=279 x=317 R10.8] is extracted from existing archives.
[y=359 x=317 B10.2] Sources: Books, research articles,
[y=375 x=317 B10.2] official reports, web statistics, online
[y=390 x=317 B10.2] databases.
