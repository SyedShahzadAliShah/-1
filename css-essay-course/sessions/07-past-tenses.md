# Session 7 — Tenses II: The Past System

**Module B · Time, voice, narration**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** historical examples in essays; past-tense correction; later narration backshift

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Form the four **past** tenses and *used to*.
2. Place a dated CSS example (*in 1971, in 1998, after the 18th Amendment*) in **past simple**.
3. Use **past perfect** only when one past event is earlier than another.
4. Avoid *had went, did not went, was failed* (wrong passive).
5. Insert a 3-sentence historical illustration inside a present-tense argument without contaminating the thesis tense.

**Traps:** overusing past perfect for everything; *was* + past participle when they mean active (*was failed*); mixing *used to* and *would* without a time frame.

**Stretch:** *would* for past habit after a time frame: *In the 1990s the rupee would often slide after a political shock.*

## Student notes

### 1. The four past forms

| Form | Pattern | Job | CSS example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Past simple** | *failed / went* | finished time, dated event, story | *In 2010 the floods displaced millions.* |
| **Past continuous** | *was/were + -ing* | background; interrupted action | *While the House **was debating**, the ordinance lapsed.* |
| **Past perfect** | *had + past participle* | earlier of two pasts | *By the time the aid arrived, the crop **had failed**.* |
| **Past perfect continuous** | *had been + -ing* | duration before a past point | *Farmers **had been waiting** for water for six weeks when the canal opened.* |

Markers for **past simple**: *yesterday, last year, in 1999, after the war, ago, that morning*.

### 2. Past simple is the narrative engine

Form:

- all persons: *failed, went, had, was/were*  
- negative: *did not fail* (base form after *did*)  
- question: *Did the state fail?*

Irregulars must be automatic: *became, began, brought, built, chose, dealt, felt, found, led, lost, made, meant, paid, said, sold, sent, spent, stood, took, thought, won, wrote*.

### 3. Past perfect — only when you need “earlier”

Do not sprinkle *had* to look formal.

Use past perfect when the sentence contains **two past times** and you must mark which came first:

- *The government announced a relief package because the flood **had destroyed** the crop.*  
  (destruction → then announcement)

If the order is already clear from *after / before / then*, past simple may be enough:

- *After the flood destroyed the crop, the government announced relief.*

**Wrong CSS solemnity:** *Pakistan had got independence in 1947.*  
**Right:** *Pakistan **got** / **gained** independence in 1947.* (one dated event)

### 4. Past continuous

- *The regulator **was sleeping** when the crash happened.* (background)  
- Not for a completed dated fact: *In 1998 Pakistan **was testing** nuclear devices* is possible as scene-setting; *Pakistan **tested** nuclear devices in 1998* is the clean essay fact.

### 5. used to / would

- *used to + base*: past habit or state, now finished. *The rupee used to be pegged.*  
- *would + base*: past habit of **action**, not state. *Every summer the canal **would** run dry.* Not: *The rupee would be pegged.* (state → *used to*)

Do not confuse with *be used to + -ing* (Session 3).

### 6. Inserting history inside a present argument (essay craft)

CSS essays live in the **present** (what should be done now) but borrow **past** evidence.

Pattern:

1. Present claim.  
2. Past illustration (2–3 sentences, one time frame).  
3. Present link (*This episode still matters because…*).

Example:

- *Independent statistics are a condition of adult government. In 2010 the flood losses were guessed in public because the data systems had not been funded. That guesswork still haunts budget credibility today.*

Notice: the middle is past; the first and last are present. That is **controlled** mixing, not chaos.

### 7. Passive in the past (preview of Session 10)

- *The Act **was passed** in 2010.*  
- *The Act **had been passed** before the flood.*  
Do not write *The Act was passed in 2010 by the Parliament was…* Keep one finite verb cluster per clause.

## Class drill

**A. Choose**

1. In 1973 Pakistan (adopts / adopted / has adopted / had adopted) a new constitution.  
2. By 1973 the assembly (debated / had debated) the draft for years.  
3. While the House (debated / was debating) the clause, a walk-out began.  
4. After the 18th Amendment (passed / had passed / was passing), several ministries (moved / had moved) to the provinces. (Write the most natural pair.)  
5. She (used to / was used to) write a page daily when she was a student.

**B. Repair**

6. He did not wrote the outline.  
7. The policy had failed in 2019. (only one past time given)  
8. They had built the dam last year.  
9. I was knowing the result yesterday.  
10. Pakistan is testing devices in 1998.

**C. Timeline** — put the verbs in the right past form.

Events: (i) drought lasts two years (ii) crop fails (iii) cabinet meets.

11. When the cabinet met, the crop ___ (fail) because a drought ___ (last) two years.

## Essay-transfer task

Thesis (present): **Energy policy needs independence from daily politics.**

Write **150 words** in three stacked blocks, labelled:

- **A. Claim** (3–4 sentences, present system)  
- **B. Illustration** (3 sentences, past system, include one year or one named episode: circular debt, load-shedding years, a specific plant delay — keep it modest and factual in tone)  
- **C. Link** (2 sentences, back to present perfect or present simple)

Underline every verb. In the margin write *pres* or *past*.

## Homework

2. 8 sentences of past perfect that actually need it (two past times).  
3. 80 words: a historical illustration for *Federalism in Pakistan is unfinished business* — past tense only, then one present link sentence.

## Exit ticket

1. When is past perfect necessary?  
2. Correct: *He did not went to the centre.*  
3. *used to* vs *be used to* — one sentence each.  
4. Why is *Pakistan had got independence in 1947* poor CSS English?

## Teacher mark pointers

- Item 1: *adopted*.  
- Item 7: *The policy failed in 2019* (drop *had*).  
- Item 11: *had failed* + *had lasted* (both earlier than *met*), or *failed* + *had lasted*. Accept *had failed / had lasted*.  
- Transfer: if block B leaks into present, mark T. If block A is entirely past, the thesis died — mark T.
