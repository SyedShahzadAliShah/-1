# Session 4 — Parts of Speech III: Adjectives, Adverbs, Determiners

**Module A · Foundation**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** diction in the essay; comparison errors in sentence correction (*prefer to, senior to, more better*)

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Place an adjective before a noun or after a linking verb (*is, seems, becomes*).
2. Form comparatives and superlatives, including *to* after *senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer*.
3. Choose *few / a few / little / a little / much / many*.
4. Stop stacking empty intensifiers (*very, highly, crystal clear, utmost*).
5. Turn a vague evaluative sentence into a precise one.

## Student notes

### 1. Adjective vs adverb

- **Adjective** describes a **noun** or pronoun: *a weak regulator, the policy is weak*.  
- **Adverb** describes a **verb, adjective, or other adverb**: *the regulator acted weakly; a surprisingly weak regulator*.

After linking verbs (*be, seem, appear, become, remain, look, feel*), use an adjective:

- *The argument **seems sound**.* (not *soundly*)  
- *The data **look reliable**.* (not *reliably*)

Manner of action uses an adverb:

- *She argued **soundly**.*  
- *He writes **well**.* (*good* is the adjective: *a good brief*)

### 2. Irregular pair that CSS students miss

| Adjective | Adverb | Danger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| good | well | *he did good* → *he did well* / *he did good work* |
| fast | fast | no *fastly* |
| hard | hard | *hardly* means *almost not* |
| late | late | *lately* means *recently* |
| high | high / highly | *highly* = very (opinions): *highly likely* |
| near | near / nearly | *nearly* = almost |

- *The committee worked **hard**.* (effort)  
- *The committee **hardly** worked.* (almost no work) — opposite meanings.

### 3. Comparison

| Degree | Short adj. | Long adj. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive | *weak* | *effective* |
| Comparative (two) | *weaker than* | *more effective than* |
| Superlative (three+) | *the weakest* | *the most effective* |

Never double: *more weaker, most weakest, more better, more easier*.

**Latin / fixed *to* (not *than*)**

- *prefer X **to** Y*  
- *senior / junior / superior / inferior / prior **to***  
- *preferable **to***

*A career in the police is not inferior **to** a career in the PAS.*  
Not: *inferior than*.

**as…as** for equality: *This year’s flood was as severe **as** 2010.*  
*not so/as…as* for inequality.

Uncountable vs countable comparison:

- *less inflation, fewer jobs*  
- *less time, fewer hours*

### 4. Determiners of quantity (CSS gold)

| Word | Use | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **few** | countable | almost none (negative) |
| **a few** | countable | some (positive) |
| **little** | uncountable | almost none (negative) |
| **a little** | uncountable | some (positive) |
| **many** | countable | |
| **much** | uncountable (mostly questions/negatives; in essays prefer *a great deal of / little*) | |
| **several** | countable, more than two | |
| **each / every** | singular | *each province has* |

- *Few reformers survive the first year.* (grim)  
- *A few reformers survived the first year.* (there were some)

### 5. Empty intensifiers: the fake CSS style

These words are not wrong. They are **cheap** when they replace evidence.

Avoid stacking: *very highly extremely important huge crisis*.

| Cheap | Sharper |
| --- | --- |
| a very big problem | a fiscal gap equal to X / a gap the size of the education budget |
| crystal clear | evident / the figures show |
| utmost importance | indispensable / legally required |
| huge / massive / gigantic | name the scale |
| unique | *unique* already means one of a kind — no *very unique* |
| perfect | no *more perfect* |

A CSS examiner has read *the burning issue of the day* five thousand times. Write the issue.

### 6. Adjectives as a class of people

*the poor, the rich, the unemployed, the young* take a **plural** verb:

- *The poor **pay** a higher share of indirect tax.*

### 7. Hyphenated modifiers

Before a noun, hyphenate a two-word description:

- *a three-year plan, a well-known reformer, a long-term risk*  
After the noun: *the plan is three years old; the reformer is well known.*

| Don’t | Do |
| --- | --- |
| *She writes good.* | *She writes well.* / *Her writing is good.* |
| *more better / more worse* | *better / worse* |
| *prefer tea than coffee* | *prefer tea to coffee* |
| *He is senior than me.* | *He is senior to me.* |
| *very unique* | *unique* |
| *too much problems* | *too many problems* |

## Class drill

**A. Choose the right form**

1. The briefing was (clear / clearly). The officer spoke (clear / clearly).  
2. She worked (hard / hardly) on the précis; she (hard / hardly) slept.  
3. There are (few / a few / little) honest brokers in the market. (Meaning: some exist.)  
4. There is (few / little / a little) time left before the monsoon. (Meaning: almost none.)  
5. Islamabad is (colder / more colder) than Karachi in January.

**B. Repair**

6. I prefer governance than slogans.  
7. This solution is more better.  
8. He ran fastly towards the court.  
9. Every provinces have a complaint.  
10. The poor is ignored in indirect taxation.  
11. She is junior than the secretary.  
12. We have too much files and too many work.

## Essay-transfer task

Here are eight **cheap** sentences. Rewrite each in **one** cleaner sentence. Add a fact-shaped phrase (it may be invented for practice, but keep it plausible and modest — we are practising language, not fake research).

1. Corruption is a very big problem in Pakistan.  
2. Education is highly important for the youth.  
3. Climate change is the most unique challenge.  
4. The government must take huge steps.  
5. It is crystal clear that inflation hurts the poor.  
6. Social media has a massive impact.  
7. Water is of utmost importance.  
8. Unemployment is a burning issue of the day.

## Homework

1. Write 12 comparative sentences using: *prefer to, senior to, inferior to, fewer, less, as…as, the most, better, worse, harder, more effective, the least*.  
2. 100 words on *A free press is indispensable to democracy* — you may use *very* **zero** times.  
3. Collect 10 adjectives from a Dawn editorial. For each, write the adverb if it exists.  
4. Error log: intensifiers you overuse.

---

## Exit ticket

1. *hard* vs *hardly* in two short sentences.  
2. Correct: *I prefer coffee than tea.*  
3. *few* or *a few*: you want to sound hopeful about reformers.  
4. Why is *very unique* illogical?

---

## Teacher mark pointers

- Transfer task: reward **deletion of intensifier + addition of a concrete noun**. Do not reward a longer sentence that is still empty.  
- Item 12: *too many files and too much work*.  
- Item 10: *The poor **are** ignored*.  
- Accept *clearly* for both A1 if a student treats *briefing* as how it was delivered; the intended pair is adjective then adverb. Explain, don’t over-penalise.
