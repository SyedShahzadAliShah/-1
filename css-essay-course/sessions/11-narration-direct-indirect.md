# Session 11 — Narration: Direct and Indirect Speech

**Module B · Time, voice, narration**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** narration items in older papers; quoting reports and thinkers in the essay without tense collapse

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Convert statements, questions, and commands from **direct** to **indirect** speech.
2. Apply **backshift** when the reporting verb is past, and know when not to.
3. Change pronouns, time/place words, and question order.
4. Choose a precise **reporting verb** (*argued, warned, conceded, estimated*).
5. Embed one short quotation in a CSS paragraph without turning the paragraph into a scrapbook.

---

**Traps:** *said me*; keeping the question mark in indirect; *He asked that what*; no backshift after *said*; quoting three poets in 100 words.

**Stretch:** reporting verbs of argument: *allege, concede, refute, contend, testify*.

## Student notes

### 1. Why a CSS essay needs narration

You will mention a report, a judgment, a speech, or a writer. You have two honest tools:

- **Direct speech:** the exact words, in quotation marks. Use **rarely**, and only when the wording itself matters.  
- **Indirect speech (narration):** you report the meaning as a *that*-clause. This is the default in academic essays.

A CSS script stuffed with boxed quotes looks like a cram academy product. One well-framed line is enough.

### 2. The reporting frame

- *say* + *that* (no personal object required): *The minister **said that** the debt was falling.*  
- *tell* + **object** + *that*: *The minister **told the House that** the debt was falling.*  
- Never: *said me / said the House that*.  
- *said to the House that* is possible; *told the House* is cleaner.

Choose a verb that already contains your attitude:

| Neutral | Sharper |
| --- | --- |
| said, told, stated | warned, admitted, claimed, alleged, estimated, insisted, denied, urged, conceded |

*The IMF **warned** that reserves were thin* is stronger than *The IMF said that…*

### 3. Four changes (when the reporting verb is **past**)

#### (i) Pronouns

Shift so they match the reporter’s world.

- He said, “**I** have **my** file.” → He said that **he** had **his** file.

#### (ii) Tense backshift

| Direct | Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| am / is | was |
| are | were |
| have / has | had |
| present simple | past simple |
| present continuous | past continuous |
| present perfect | past perfect |
| past simple | past perfect |
| past perfect | past perfect (no further back) |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must | must / had to |
| shall (I/we) | would |

#### (iii) Time and place

| Direct | Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last week | the previous week |
| next week | the following week |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |

If the report is written **on the same day** and the words are still live, some markers stay. In classroom keys, apply the table unless the stem says “same day”.

#### (iv) Joiners and punctuation

Drop quotation marks. Drop the question mark and the exclamation mark in indirect speech.

- Statement: *that*  
- Yes/no question: *if / whether*  
- Wh-question: keep *what / why / how / where / when / who*  
- Command: *to + verb* / *not to + verb*

### 4. Questions: statement word order

This is the item students lose.

Direct: *He said, “Where **does** the secretary **live**?”*  
Indirect: *He asked where the secretary **lived**.*  
Not: *He asked where did the secretary live.*  
Not: *He asked that where the secretary lived.*

Direct: *She said, “**Are** you ready?”*  
Indirect: *She asked **if** I **was** ready.*

### 5. Commands, requests, advice

- “Sit down.” → *He told me **to sit** down.*  
- “Please wait.” → *He asked me **to wait**.*  
- “Don’t leak the file.” → *He ordered me **not to leak** the file.*  
- “You should publish it.” → *He advised me **to publish** it.* / *He said that I should publish it.*

### 6. When **not** to backshift

1. Reporting verb is **present / present perfect / future**: *The report **says** that the aquifer **is** falling.*  
2. Universal truth or still-operative law (optional, if you want to mark it as still true): *He said that the Constitution **guarantees** dignity.*  
3. Past simple with a **named date** sometimes stays: *He said that Pakistan tested devices **in 1998**.* (the date already locks the past)

### 7. Punctuating a rare direct quote in an essay

- *The World Bank noted, “Pakistan’s water economy is at a breaking point.”*  
- Comma (or colon) before the quote. Capital inside if it is a full sentence. Source **before** or **immediately after**.  
- After the quote, return to **your** present-tense argument. Do not let the quote’s tense infect the next three lines.

### 8. Never let narration become decoration

One report, one thinker, or one judgment per paragraph is enough. Then **your** analysis. Examiners have said quotations must be well placed and relevant.

## Urdu-interference box

- *Us ne kaha ke kya tum tayyar ho* → not *He said that are you ready* → *He asked if I was ready.*  
- *Us ne mujh se kaha* → *He **told** me*, not *said me*.  
- *Pooha ke kahan rehta hai* → *asked where he lived*, not *asked that where does he live*.

## Class drill

**A. Indirect (reporting verb past)**

1. She said, “I am drafting the rules.”  
2. He said to me, “I completed the file yesterday.”  
3. The minister said, “We will publish the debt.”  
4. She said, “I can verify the names.”  
5. He said, “Water boils at 100°C.” (keep the science true)

**B. Questions and commands**

6. He said, “Are the rolls ready?”  
7. She said, “Where does the canal leak?”  
8. The judge said, “Produce the record.”  
9. The secretary said, “Do not brief the press.”  
10. Repair: He asked that what was the delay.

**C. Essay frame**

11. Turn into one indirect sentence with a sharp verb: World Bank / “Pakistan’s water economy is at a breaking point.” (treat as a past report)

## Essay-transfer task

Theme: **Climate reports are not optional reading for a state that lives on the monsoon.**

Write **140 words**.

Must include:

1. Your thesis in present simple.  
2. **One** direct quotation (maximum 15 words, invented-for-practice or from a well-known public line — label it *practice quote* if invented). Correct punctuation.  
3. **One** indirect sentence reporting a finding (*A 20xx assessment warned that…*).  
4. Two sentences of **your** analysis in the present system.

If you cannot remember a real report, write *A national assessment warned that urban aquifers were falling* — honest generic reporting is better than a fake exact citation.

---

## Homework

1. Convert 8 mixed items (3 statements, 3 questions, 2 commands) from a mixed list you write yourself about *education*.  
2. Punctuate: *the chief justice said the law will find you* — write both a legal direct version and an indirect version.  
3. 80 words: report a classroom debate using only **indirect** speech and three different reporting verbs.  
4. Error log: *said me*, question-order, missed backshift.

---

## Exit ticket

1. *said* vs *told* — one line.  
2. Indirect: “Where do you live?” he asked me.  
3. Why drop the question mark in indirect speech?  
4. When do you refuse to backshift?

## Teacher mark pointers

- 2: *He told me that he had completed the file the previous day.* (*said to me that* acceptable.)  
- 5: *boils* preferred.  
- 6: *He asked if/whether the rolls were ready.*  
- 10: *He asked what the delay was.*  
- 11: *The World Bank warned that Pakistan’s water economy was at a breaking point.* (past report) or *has warned that … is* if you treat the report as still current — both defensible; the student must be consistent.  
- Invented quotes in the transfer must be labelled if not real. We are teaching grammar, not fabricating sources for the real exam. Tell students: in the real CSS paper, only quote what you can actually attribute.
