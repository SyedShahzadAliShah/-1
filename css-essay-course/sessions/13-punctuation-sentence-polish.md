# Session 13 — Punctuation and Sentence Polish

**Module C · Accuracy and transfer**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** 2–3 punctuation items in sentence correction; readability of the essay

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Use the **full stop, comma, semicolon, colon, apostrophe, quotation marks, hyphen, dash, question mark**.
2. Identify and kill the **comma splice**.
3. Punctuate *however, therefore, for example* correctly.
4. Control *its / it’s, Pakistan’s, 1990s, MPs*.
5. Punctuate a short dialogue-free academic paragraph and a single quotation.

---

## Teacher plan (90 minutes)

| Time | Move |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Recap *If I were / had + would have*. |
| 8–28 | Marks one by one, 90 seconds each, with a CSS example. Comma splice as enemy #1. |
| 28–50 | Unpunctuated paragraph + 8 exam-style punctuation repairs. |
| 50–75 | Transfer: punctuate a dirty 160-word essay opening on *A free press*; then rewrite two splices as (a) two sentences (b) semicolon (c) *because*. |
| 75–85 | Apostrophe clinic: *its, 2000s, officers’ hostel, FBR’s*. |
| 85–90 | Homework, exit. |

**Traps:** comma after *although* clause missing (minor) vs comma splice (major); *however* as a conjunction; *it’s* in an essay; colon vs semicolon mix-up.

**Stretch:** paired dashes for an interruption; no space before semicolon/colon in standard BrE typing.

## Student notes

### 1. Punctuation is grammar you can hear

If you would take a full breath, you probably need a **full stop**, not a comma.

FPSC often puts 2–3 punctuation-only items in sentence correction. They also silently punish punctuation in the essay because it is “flawed language.”

### 2. Full stop (.)

Ends a statement. CSS default. When in doubt, stop.

### 3. Comma (,)

Use a comma:

1. After a front dependent clause: *Although the law exists, it is unenforced.*  
2. Before a coordinator joining two **full** sentences: *The law exists, but it is unenforced.*  
3. In a list: *tax, spend and explain* (BrE often skips the extra comma before *and*; a serial comma is not a fault).  
4. Around extra information: *The 18th Amendment, which recast the federation, still lacks a fiscal follow-through.*  
5. After a sentence adverb: *However, the rolls are dirty.*  
6. In numbers (BrE): *1,000,000*  
7. With a reporting frame: *The court held, “The right is not absolute.”*

Do **not** use a comma:

- to join two full sentences (**comma splice**)  
- after *that* in *He said that the bill had failed.*  
- between subject and verb: *The quality of the reforms, is poor.*

**Comma splice (illegal):**  
*Inflation is high, the poor suffer.*

Legal repairs:

- *Inflation is high. The poor suffer.*  
- *Inflation is high; the poor suffer.*  
- *Inflation is high, **and** the poor suffer.*  
- *Inflation is high **because** the poor’s food basket is import-heavy.* (recast)

### 4. Semicolon (;)

Joins two **related complete** sentences without a coordinator.

- *The law exists; enforcement does not.*

Also separates messy list items that already contain commas.

Do not use a semicolon before *and/but* in ordinary student writing. Do not use it after *although*.

### 5. Colon (:)

Introduces an explanation, a list, or a quotation after a **complete** lead-in.

- *The state faces a simple test: can it tax its friends?*  
- *Three files are missing: the annexure, the minutes and the dissenting note.*

Wrong: *The state faces: inflation and debt.* (the lead-in is not complete)

### 6. Apostrophe (’)

| Job | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Possession, singular | *Pakistan’s economy, the court’s order* |
| Possession, plural already ending in *s* | *the provinces’ demand, the officers’ hostel* |
| Irregular plural | *the children’s share, the people’s house* |
| Contractions | *it’s = it is* — **avoid contractions in CSS essays** |
| Missing letters | don’t use *gov’t* |

**its** = possessive of *it* (no apostrophe): *The state lost **its** nerve.*  
**it’s** = it is. Do not write *it’s* in the essay. Write *it is* or recast.

Decades: *the 1990s* (no apostrophe). Not *1990’s* unless you mean *the 1990’s budget* (awkward — write *the 1990 budget*).

Abbreviations: *MPs, NGOs* (plural, no apostrophe). *the MP’s question* (one MP, possessive).

### 7. Quotation marks

British school style often uses **single** quotes; many Pakistani papers use **double**. This course uses **double** for CSS scripts because they scan clearly in handwritten papers.

- One short quote, source attached.  
- Comma/colon before a full-sentence quote.  
- Full stop **inside** if the quote is a full sentence: *He said, “The debt is structural.”*

### 8. Hyphen vs dash

- **Hyphen** (-) builds one adjective: *a long-term risk, a well-known judgment, a three-year delay*.  
- **Dash** (—) (in handwriting, two hyphens or a long stroke) marks a break: *The census — delayed again — has poisoned the NFC.* Use rarely.

### 9. Question mark and exclamation

Ask a real question: *Can a state that fears data claim adult government?*  
Do not pepper the essay with rhetorical questions. **No exclamation marks** in CSS essays.

### 10. *however, therefore, moreover, for example*

These are **not** FANBOYS. They cannot splice.

Wrong: *The law exists, however it is dead.*  
Right: *The law exists. However, it is dead.* / *The law exists; however, it is dead.* / *The law exists but is dead.*

## Urdu-interference box

Urdu punctuation is lighter. Students produce English “and-and-and” chains with commas:

- *Pakistan is an agricultural country, most people live in villages, they have no hospitals, the youth is frustrated.*  
  Four sentences. Put stops. Then keep **one** link: *Pakistan remains largely agrarian. Most villagers still lack a functioning hospital, and frustrated youth leave for the city.*

## Class drill

**A. Repair only punctuation (smallest change).**

1. Inflation is high, the poor suffer.  
2. The law exists, however it is unenforced.  
3. Although the rolls are dirty the petition failed.  
4. The state faces: debt inflation and a thin tax base.  
5. Its a long delay in the census.  
6. The 1990’s were kinder to the rupee.  
7. The officers hostel is full. (more than one officer)  
8. Can a fearful state claim adult government.

**B. Choose semicolon or colon.**

9. The test is simple ___ tax your friends.  
10. The test is simple ___ the state still fails it.

**C. Unpunctuated block** — punctuate without rewriting words:

11. although the 18th amendment recast the federation fiscal follow through remains weak the provinces demand money they do not always build capacity however the answer is not to reverse devolution it is to audit it

## Essay-transfer task

Dirty opening (copy, then punctuate, then polish two splices in three legal ways):

*a free press is not a luxury it is a checking institution the state often treats journalists as a nuisance however a state that cannot bear news cannot bear citizens the constitution names expression as a right the practice names it as a risk*

Then continue with **80 words of your own**, fully punctuated, no contractions, one optional colon, no exclamation.

## Homework

1. Copy ten sentences from Dawn. Mark every punctuation. Note one you would have got wrong.  
2. Write 8 comma-splice repairs, each splice repaired **three** ways (stop / semicolon / conjunction).  
3. Apostrophe sheet: 12 possessives including *its, people’s, provinces’, FBR’s, 2000s*.  
4. Error log.

## Exit ticket

1. Three legal repairs of a comma splice.  
2. *its* vs *it’s*.  
3. Why can’t *however* do the job of *but*?  
4. Punctuate: *the court held the right is not absolute* (as a quotation).

## Teacher mark pointers

- 3: comma after *dirty* is preferred.  
- 4: *The state faces three shocks: debt, inflation and a thin tax base.* (may add *three shocks* to make the lead-in complete) or recast without colon. Smallest change: *The state faces debt, inflation and a thin tax base.*  
- 5: *It’s* → *It is* / *Its* is wrong here because it means *it is*.  
- 6: *The 1990s*.  
- 7: *The officers’ hostel*.  
- 9: colon. 10: semicolon.  
- Reward students who use fewer marks correctly rather than more marks fashionably.
