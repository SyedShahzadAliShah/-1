# Session 9 — Agreement and Sentence Architecture

**Module B · Time, voice, narration**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** sentence correction (highest-yield family); long CSS sentences that lose the verb

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Find the **head noun** and make the verb agree, even when phrases intervene.
2. Apply special patterns: *each/every, either/or, neither/nor, many a, one of, as well as, with, along with*.
3. Keep lists **parallel**.
4. Control sentence length: 12–28 words as the CSS comfort zone.
5. Repair a 40-word wreck without changing the writer’s meaning.

---

## Teacher plan (90 minutes)

| Time | Move |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Recap sequence: *said that + would*. |
| 8–28 | SVA map. Distance subjects. Nearer-subject rule. Collective nouns (BrE). Parallelism. |
| 28–50 | 12 exam-style corrections. Students must **name** the head noun before they write the verb. |
| 50–72 | Transfer: one complex sentence (max 28 words) + one compound + one simple on *The quality of governance, not the quantity of ministries, decides service delivery.* Then expand to 120 words without breaking agreement. |
| 72–85 | Mid-course warning: next session starts with the 50-mark test **or** assign the test as homework tonight / first 25 min of Session 10. (See assessments.) |
| 85–90 | Homework, exit. |

**Traps:** verb agreeing with the nearest noun; *the people is*; *many a students*; *as well as* treated like *and*.

**Stretch:** *none of* + uncountable singular / + plural countable — both seen; teach *None of the evidence **is***; *None of the reports **are/is*** — FPSC often wants *is* with *none*. This course: *none of the evidence is*; *none of the ministers **has*** (exam-safe singular).

## Student notes

### 1. Agreement is a hunt for the subject

The verb agrees with the **subject**, not with a noun that happens to sit next to the verb.

- *The **quality** of the apples **is** poor.*  
- *The **risks** in the energy sector **are** political.*  
- *A **list** of demands **has** been sent.*

Cross out prepositional phrases with a pencil: *of the apples, in the energy sector, of demands*. What is left is the subject.

### 2. Singular by rule, even when they “feel” plural

| Trigger | Verb | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, anyone* | singular | *Each of the provinces **has** a grievance.* |
| *one, another, either, neither* (as pronouns) | singular | *Neither **is** ready.* |
| *much, little, a little* | singular | *Little **has** been done.* |
| *many a + singular noun* | singular | *Many a **reformer has** quit.* |
| time, money, distance as a lump | singular | *Ten years **is** a long delay.* / *Five billion rupees **was** spent.* |
| the number of | singular | *The number of voters **is** rising.* |
| uncountable | singular | *The news **is** bad. Progress **is** slow.* |

### 3. Plural by rule

| Trigger | Verb | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *a number of, a group of* (often) | plural | *A number of files **are** missing.* |
| *few, many, several, both* | plural | *Few **are** willing.* |
| *the police* (BrE), *the poor*, *the British* | plural | *The police **are** investigating.* |
| *people* (human beings) | plural | *The people **are** ahead of the elite.* |
| nouns like *scissors, riches, thanks* | plural | *His thanks **were** brief.* |

*Peoples* = nations: *the peoples of South Asia*. Ordinary citizens = *people*.

### 4. either…or / neither…nor — nearer subject

The verb follows the subject **closest to it**.

- *Neither the ministers nor the **PM is** willing.*  
- *Neither the PM nor the **ministers are** willing.*  
- *Either the data or the **testimony is** false.*  
- *Either the testimony or the **data are** false.* (if you treat *data* as plural)

In an essay, if the pair looks ugly, recast: *The PM and the ministers are unwilling.*

### 5. *and* vs *as well as / along with / together with / in addition to / with*

- X **and** Y → usually **plural**: *The secretary and the minister **have** signed.*  
- X **as well as** Y → verb follows **X**: *The secretary as well as the minister **has** signed.*  
- If X and Y are one idea: *Bread and butter **is** her breakfast.* Rare in CSS; don’t force it.

Two titles, one person: *The secretary and spokesman **has** arrived* (one human). Two people: *The secretary and **the** spokesman **have** arrived.*

### 6. Relative clauses

The verb in *who/that/which* agrees with the **noun described**.

- *It is I who **am** responsible.* (grammatically pure; in CSS recast: *I am responsible.*)  
- *He is one of the officers who **have** signed.* (*who* → officers)  
- *He is the only one of the officers who **has** signed.* (*who* → the only one)

This pair is an FPSC favourite.

### 7. Parallelism (architecture)

When you list, keep the same grammatical shape.

- *The state must **tax**, **spend** and **explain**.*  
- Not: *tax, spending and the explanation of policy.*  
- *not only **a failure of law** but also **a failure of nerve***  
- *both **to audit** and **to publish***

Broken parallelism is a structure fault (code **S**).

### 8. Length and architecture for CSS

A readable essay sentence is often **12–28 words**. Past 35, agreement dies and so does the examiner.

Tools to shorten:

1. split with a full stop  
2. kill *It is important to note that*  
3. turn a relative clause into an adjective: *the delayed census*  
4. one idea per sentence; extra idea → extra sentence

**Wreck:** *The quality of the reforms which were introduced by the ministry in a hurry after the flood and without consultation of the provinces are doubtful.*  
**Head noun:** *quality* → **is**. Then split: *The ministry introduced hurried reforms after the flood without consulting the provinces. Their quality is doubtful.*

## Urdu-interference box

Urdu verbs often agree with the nearest noun or with a plural sense of *log*.

- *Aik bunyadi wajohat yeh hain* → *One of the basic **reasons is***  
- *Hukumat ke iqdamat nakam hai* → *The government’s **measures have** failed.* (measures = plural)

## Class drill

Name the **head subject** first, then the verb.

1. The quality of these reports (is / are) uneven.  
2. Each of the secretaries (has / have) a veto.  
3. Many a candidate (fail / fails) because of grammar.  
4. The secretary as well as the aides (was / were) late.  
5. Neither the notes nor the outline (was / were) ready.  
6. Neither the outline nor the notes (was / were) ready.  
7. He is one of those writers who (revises / revise) daily.  
8. He is the only one of those writers who (revises / revise) daily.  
9. Five years (is / are) a long time to wait for a census.  
10. A number of amendments (has / have) been dropped.  
11. The number of amendments (has / have) been dropped.  
12. Repair parallelism: *The commission was asked to examine the files, interviewing witnesses and a report.*

## Essay-transfer task

Stem: **The quality of governance, not the quantity of ministries, decides service delivery.**

1. Write the stem as a **simple** sentence (you may keep it almost as is).  
2. Write a **compound** sentence (two independents) that agrees throughout.  
3. Write a **complex** sentence (max 28 words) with *although* or *because*.  
4. Expand into **120 words**. After writing, circle every subject and every verb. Draw a line from subject to verb.

If any pair disagrees, repair before you hand in.

## Exit ticket

1. Why is *The quality of the reforms are poor* wrong?  
2. *Many a* takes singular or plural noun?  
3. Nearer-subject rule applies to which pair of correlatives?  
4. Repair: *not only corrupt but also a failure of design* (make parallel).
