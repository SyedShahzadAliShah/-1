# Session 12 — Conditionals and Hypothetical Policy

**Module C · Accuracy and transfer**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** *if/unless/until* sentence correction; policy arguments (“If Pakistan invested…”)

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Form conditionals 0, 1, 2, 3 and one mixed type.
2. Use *were* in unreal present (*If I were*).
3. Avoid *unless…not*, *until…not*, and *if it will*.
4. Match the conditional to the essay job: law of nature, real policy, imagined reform, missed chance.
5. Write a 4-sentence policy ladder on one CSS theme.

---

| Time | Move |
| --- | --- |

**Traps:** *If I would be the PM*; *If I will*; *would* in both clauses; *unless you don’t*.

**Stretch:** inverted forms *Had the census been on time, …* / *Were the rolls clean, …* — academic and useful in a conclusion.

## Student notes

### 1. A conditional is a deal between two clauses

- **If-clause** (condition)  
- **Main clause** (result)

Tense pairing is fixed. Do not invent a fifth mix in a CSS paper.

### 2. The four working types

| Type | If-clause | Main clause | Essay job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0** real, always | present | present | laws, definitions, mechanical results |
| **1** real future | present | *will / can / may / must* | practical policy, likely result |
| **2** unreal now | past (*were* for *be*) | *would / could / might* + base | imagining a different present |
| **3** unreal past | *had + past participle* | *would have / could have / might have + PP* | missed chance, regret, historical counterfactual |

Examples on one theme — water:

- 0: *If canal water is free at the margin, farmers flood the field.*  
- 1: *If the provinces meter tube wells next year, urban tankers will lose their market.*  
- 2: *If water were priced honestly, the crop mix would change.*  
- 3: *If the aquifer had been metered in the 1990s, several cities would not have sunk their own wells.*

### 3. Type 2: *were*

For unreal present, *be* becomes *were* for all persons in careful English:

- *If I **were** the finance minister, I would publish the debt.*  
- *If the federation **were** serious, the census would not slip.*

*If I was* is common in speech. CSS keys still like *were*. Use *were*.

### 4. Mixed conditional (very useful in essays)

**Past condition → present result**

- *If the census **had been** held on time, the NFC **would** be less toxic **now**.*

Pattern: *If + had + PP , would + base*.

### 5. unless / until / as long as / provided that

- **unless** = *if not*. Already negative. Do **not** add *not / never / don’t*.  
  *The bill will fail **unless** the treasury is present.*  
  Not: *unless the treasury is not present.*

- **until** = up to the time when. Use a **positive** verb for the event you wait for.  
  *Wait **until** the House **sits**.*  
  Not: *until the House does not sit.*  
  Negative result lives in the other clause: *The bill **will not** move until the House sits.*

- **as long as / provided that / providing that** = only if.  
  *The IMF programme will hold **provided that** the tax take rises.*

- **in case** = because perhaps. *Publish the rolls **in case** the court asks.* Not a synonym of *if* in every sentence.

### 6. Illegal doubles

| Don’t | Do |
| --- | --- |
| If I **would** be PM, I would… | If I **were** PM, I would… |
| If she **would have** studied, she would have passed | If she **had** studied, she would have passed |
| If it **will** rain… | If it **rains**… |
| unless you **don’t** | unless you |
| If the state **would** meter, prices **will**… | mix is illegal; pick type 1 or 2 |

Both clauses of type 2 take the “unreal” pair (*past* + *would*). Both clauses of type 3 take the “unreal past” pair (*had* + *would have*). Do not put *would* in the *if*-clause (except the rare *if you would* = willingness — skip in CSS).

### 7. Inversion (optional polish)

- *Had the rolls been clean, the petition would have failed.*  
- *Were the tax base broader, rates could be lower.*  
- *Should the monsoon fail, food inflation will bite.* (type 1, formal)

Use **one** inverted conditional in a conclusion, not in every paragraph.

### 8. *wish / as if / as though*

- *I wish the census **were** honest.* (now)  
- *I wish the census **had been** honest.* (past)  
- *He speaks **as if** he **were** the regulator.* (unreal)  
- *He speaks **as if** he **knows** the file.* (maybe he does)

FPSC has tested *as if he **were** mad*.

## Urdu-interference box

- *Agar main PM hota* → *If I **were** PM*, not *If I would be PM*.  
- *Agar us ne mehnat ki hoti* → *If she **had** worked*, not *If she would have worked*.  
- *Jab tak wo nahi aata* in English is often *until he comes*, not *until he does not come*.

## Class drill

**A. Name the type and repair if needed.**

1. If you heat ice, it melts.  
2. If the FBR will document retailers, the deficit will shrink.  
3. If I was you, I would rewrite the outline. (make it CSS-careful)  
4. If the dam was completed in 1998, the 2010 flood would have been milder. (intended: missed past)  
5. If she had published the figures, the debate would be saner now.

**B. unless / until**

6. The court will not sit unless the bar is not on strike.  
7. Do not file until you do not have the annexures. (intended: wait until you have them)  
8. Unless you study, you will not pass. (already correct? yes/no)

**C. Write to order**

9. Type 1: climate / Karachi drains.  
10. Type 3: a delayed census.

## Essay-transfer task

Theme: **Public education is the only scalable equaliser.**

Write **four standalone sentences**, labelled 0, 1, 2, 3, all on this theme.

Then write **120 words** that are **mostly type 1** (real policy) with **one** type-2 sentence (imagined present) and **one** mixed sentence (past failure → present pain).

Circle *if / unless / until / were / had*.

## Homework

1. 12 sentences: 3 of each type, two themes (gender justice, tax).  
2. Invert two type-3 sentences (*Had…*).  
3. Repair 8 *unless/until/if will* items from a list you create by deliberately writing them wrong first, then correcting.  
4. Error log.

## Exit ticket

1. Write the type-2 formula.  
2. Repair: *If I would have known…*  
3. Why is *unless you don’t go* wrong?  
4. Mixed conditional: past cause, present result — one sentence on the census.
