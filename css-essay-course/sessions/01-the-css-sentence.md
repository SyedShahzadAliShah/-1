# Session 1 — The CSS Sentence and the Examiner’s Demand

**Module A · Foundation**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** Essay paper (articulation, expression, technical treatment of style) + every grammar question in Precis & Composition

---

## Outcomes

By the end of this session the student can:

1. State, in one sentence, what FPSC is testing in the English Essay paper.
2. Separate a **sentence**, a **fragment**, and a **run-on**.
3. Find the **subject** and the **finite verb** in a 20–30 word sentence.
4. Name the four sentence types (simple, compound, complex, compound-complex) and use two of them in a CSS paragraph.
5. Sit the diagnostic test and start an **error log**.

**Traps to kill on day one**

- The “CSS opening”: *In this contemporary era it is crystal clear that…* Ban it.
- Treating a title as a sentence: *Poverty in Pakistan.* That is a fragment.
- Comma splice: *Inflation is high, the poor suffer.*

**Stretch for strong students:** write one compound-complex sentence on the same topic without exceeding 35 words.

## Student notes

### 1. What the examiner wants

The CSS English Essay is one paper, 100 marks. You choose a topic from a wide list and write a full essay. FPSC says it will examine:

- comprehensive, research-based knowledge
- **articulation** (can you say the idea cleanly?)
- **expression** (is the English controlled?)
- **technical treatment of essay style** (outline, paragraphs, argument, close)

This 17-session course builds the last three. Knowledge without a working sentence is a long, expensive fail.

### 2. The only unit that matters: the sentence

A **sentence** is a group of words that contains:

1. a **subject** (who/what the sentence is about)
2. a **finite verb** (a verb with tense: *is, fails, passed, will reform*)
3. a **complete thought** (it can stand alone)

Examples:

- *Pakistan needs a competent civil service.*  
  Subject: *Pakistan*. Finite verb: *needs*. Complete.

- *Because Pakistan needs a competent civil service.*  
  Fragment. *Because* leaves the thought unfinished.

- *Pakistan needs a competent civil service, recruitment is politicised.*  
  Run-on (comma splice). Two sentences pretending to be one.

### 3. Finite verb vs verbal

*ing* and *to + verb* are **not** finite by themselves:

- *Controlling inflation.* → fragment  
- *Controlling inflation requires independent data.* → sentence (*requires* is finite)

### 4. Four sentence types (learn the names)

| Type | Pattern | CSS example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple** | one independent clause | *Climate stress is a security issue.* |
| **Compound** | two independents joined by *and/but/so/or/yet* or a semicolon | *Climate stress is a security issue, and water conflict will follow.* |
| **Complex** | one independent + one or more dependents (*because, although, if, which, when*) | *Although budgets exist, implementation remains weak.* |
| **Compound-complex** | at least two independents + one dependent | *Although budgets exist, implementation remains weak, and donors have noticed.* |

For CSS, **complex** is the workhorse. It shows cause, contrast, and condition. Do not stack four clauses into one monster. Examiners call that “flawed sentence structure.”

### 5. How to find the subject in a long sentence

Ignore prepositional phrases (*of the reforms, in the rural areas*).

- *The quality of the recent reforms in the energy sector **is** still doubtful.*  
  Subject = *quality*, not *reforms*, not *sector*.

### 6. Three legal ways to join complete thoughts

1. Full stop: *Inflation is high. The poor suffer.*  
2. Semicolon: *Inflation is high; the poor suffer.*  
3. Coordinator: *Inflation is high, **and** the poor suffer.*

Illegal: comma alone. Illegal: nothing at all.

### 7. Register: CSS is not a speech, not a novel

Write adult, calm, exact English.

Weak: *Our beloved country is facing a lot of problems nowadays.*  
Stronger: *Pakistan faces a fiscal and administrative crisis that the present policy mix has not eased.*

You do not need rare words. You need the **right common words** in the right order.

## Urdu-interference box

Urdu often runs clauses together with *aur* and *ke*. Direct translation produces:

- *Pakistan is an agricultural country, most people live in villages, they have no facilities.*  
  Three sentences. Put stops. Or write: *Pakistan remains largely agrarian; most citizens still live in villages with weak public services.*

## Class drill (10 minutes)

Label each item **S** (sentence), **F** (fragment), or **R** (run-on). Rewrite F and R.

1. Governance without accountability.  
2. The civil service recruits talent, it does not always retain it.  
3. When a state cannot tax fairly.  
4. Water scarcity threatens both farms and cities.  
5. Because the census was delayed the planning stayed blind.  
6. Social media spreads rumour it also spreads evidence.  
7. To reform the police overnight.  
8. Education cannot wait until the economy recovers.

## Essay-transfer task (22 minutes)

Topic: **Education is the cheapest defence of a nation.**

Write **six numbered sentences**:

- 2 simple  
- 2 compound (use *and / but / so*)  
- 2 complex (use *because / although / if*)

Underline every finite verb. Circle every subject.

Peer check: if a partner cannot find a finite verb, it is not a sentence.

## Homework (45–60 minutes)

1. Copy the Formula Sheet section 1 into your error-log notebook.  
2. From a Dawn editorial, copy **five** sentences. Label each type (simple / compound / complex / compound-complex).  
3. Rewrite the following as correct English (do not change the meaning):

   a. Poverty is increasing, government is not serious.  
   b. Although the policy is good but implementation is weak.  
   c. Pakistan facing many challenges in education sector.  
   d. Because the youth is frustrated.  
   e. Climate change is real it is already here.

4. Write 80 words on *Why a CSS essay fails*. Use at least two complex sentences. No dictionary definition.

5. Start the **error log**: three columns — *My sentence / Fault name / Corrected sentence*.

## Exit ticket (4 minutes)

1. Write one legal definition of a sentence.  
2. Correct: *Inflation is high, the poor suffer.*  
3. Is this a sentence? *While the state borrowed heavily.* Yes/No + reason.  
4. Name the four sentence types.

---

## Teacher mark pointers

- Accept British or American comma-before-*and* in a list; do not accept comma splices.  
- *Because the census was delayed the planning stayed blind* is a **sentence** (complex) even without a comma; prefer a comma after the dependent clause when it comes first.  
- Homework 3b must drop either *although* or *but*, not keep both.  
- Do not award style marks for quotations in this session. We are building the sentence unit only.
