# Session 8 — Tenses III: Future, Mixed Tenses, Sequence

**Module B · Time, voice, narration**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** policy recommendations (*will / going to / must*); sequence of tenses; *if I will*

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Form future meaning with *will, going to, present simple, present continuous, future perfect*.
2. Keep **sequence of tenses** when a past reporting verb governs a clause.
3. Mix tenses **on purpose** (present claim + past proof + future result).
4. Avoid *If it will rain* and *I will inform you when he will come*.
5. Write a closing paragraph of recommendations that is forceful but grammatically legal.

---

| Time | Move |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Recap past perfect: one board sentence with two pasts. |

| 30–50 | Repair + choose-the-future. |

| 85–90 | Homework, exit. |

**Traps:** *shall* overuse (*I shall say that…*); *will* in *if*-clauses; future continuous everywhere; sequence ignored after *said that*.

**Stretch:** future perfect: *By 2030 the aquifer will have dropped below pumping cost.*

## Student notes

### 1. English has no single “future tense”

We **express** the future in several ways. CSS needs three of them daily.

| Form | Pattern | Use in an essay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **will + base** | *will fail* | prediction; result of a policy; spontaneous decision (rare in essays) |
| **be going to** | *is going to fail* | evidence-based near future; already decided trajectory |
| **present simple** | *fails / the House meets on Monday* | timetabled event; and **all *if/when* clauses** |
| **present continuous** | *is meeting the IMF team tomorrow* | fixed personal/institutional arrangement |
| **future continuous** | *will be debating* | action in progress at a future point (rare) |
| **future perfect** | *will have failed* | completed before a future deadline: *by 2030* |
| **modal future** | *must, should, may, could* | recommendation and hedge (often better than *will*) |

CSS close: prefer **must / should** for what the state is obliged to do, and **will** for the likely consequence.

- *Pakistan **must** meter agricultural water. If it does not, cities **will** buy tankers at political prices.*

### 2. No *will* after *if / when / as soon as / until / before / after / unless* (time/condition clauses)

The **subordinate** clause takes present (or present perfect). The **main** clause may take *will*.

| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| If the monsoon **will** fail, prices will rise. | If the monsoon **fails**, prices will rise. |
| I will write when the report **will** come. | I will write when the report **comes**. |
| Until the House **will** sit, the bill will wait. | Until the House **sits**, the bill will wait. |
| If she **will** study, she passes. | If she **studies**, she **will** pass. |

Exception (advanced, optional): *if you will* = *if you are willing*. Too spoken for CSS. Skip it.

### 3. Sequence of tenses (the chain)

When the **main verb is past**, the next clause usually moves one step back. This is the same engine as narration (Session 11).

| Direct / original | After a past main verb |
| --- | --- |
| *is* | *was* |
| *are* | *were* |
| *has/have* | *had* |
| *does / present simple* | *did / past simple* |
| *will* | *would* |
| *can* | *could* |
| *may* | *might* |
| *must* | *must* or *had to* (meaning decides) |

- *The minister **says** that the debt **is** stable.* (both present — he says it now)  
- *The minister **said** that the debt **was** stable.* (he said it then)

**Do not backshift** if the statement is still timelessly true and you want to mark it as still true:

- *The teacher said that water **boils** at 100°C.*  
- *The report said that the Constitution **guarantees** dignity.* (still in force)

In CSS, for **dated claims**, backshift. For **law and nature**, present is defensible. Be consistent inside the paragraph.

### 4. Controlled mixing — the adult paragraph

A high-scoring paragraph often walks through time **once**, in order:

1. **Present** — the problem as it stands  
2. **Present perfect** — how long it has lasted  
3. **Past** — one illustration  
4. **Future / modal** — what follows if nothing changes, and what must be done

That is four tenses, one logic. Random hopping is the fault. Planned walking is style.

### 5. shall

British legal English uses *shall* for obligation in statutes. In a CSS essay, *shall* sounds theatrical (*I shall now discuss*). Use *will* for prediction and *must* for obligation.

## Urdu-interference box

Urdu future (*ga / gi / ge*) appears in both clauses. English does not.

- *Agar barish hogi to rate barh jayega* → not *If it will rain…* → *If it rains, the rate will rise.*  
- *Jab woh ayega main batata hoon* → *When he comes, I will tell you.* / *When he comes, I will let you know.*

## Class drill

**A. Repair the illegal *will***

1. If the census will slip again, the NFC will be blind.  
2. The bank will act when the inflation will peak.  
3. Unless the provinces will cooperate, the dam will stall.  
4. As soon as the House will pass the bill, the ministry will notify it.

**B. Sequence**

5. He said that the policy (is / was) a mistake. (reporting a past interview)  
6. The Constitution said nothing of the sort; it (guarantees / guaranteed) dignity — choose and justify.  
7. She promised that she (will / would) publish the figures.  
8. Scientists have shown that ice (melts / melted) at 0°C.

**C. Future form**

9. By 2030, if nothing changes, several cities (will run / will have run) out of cheap groundwater. (deadline completed)  
10. The committee (meets / will meeting / is going to meets) on Monday; the notice is already out. (timetable)

## Essay-transfer task

Prompt: **If Pakistan does not meter agricultural water, cities will run dry.**

Write **160 words** in four labelled beats:

1. Present problem (simple + continuous as needed)  
2. Present perfect duration (*since/for*)  
3. One past illustration (a year or a city)  
4. Future consequence + *must/should* recommendation  

Ban: *if … will* in the *if*-clause; *shall I say*; *need of the hour*.

---

## Homework

1. Write 8 legal *if/when/until* sentences with *will* only in the main clause.  
2. Convert: *The minister says, “The circular debt is falling.”* into past reporting with backshift, then into a version that keeps present because you believe the claim is still true. Note the difference.  
3. 100-word closing paragraph of recommendations on *urban flooding* using *must* twice, *should* once, *will* once, *may* once.  
4. Error log.

---

## Exit ticket

1. Why is *If it will rain, we will cancel* wrong?  
2. *said that she will come* — repair.  
3. Which form: completed before 2030?  
4. Name the four beats of a controlled mixed-tense paragraph.

## Teacher mark pointers

- 6: accept *guarantees* with a one-line justification (still in force). *guaranteed* is also possible if treating the text as a historical act — ask the student to be consistent.  
- 9: *will have run*.  
- 10: *meets* (timetable) or *is meeting* (arrangement). Not *will meeting*.  
- Transfer beat 4 without a named actor (*the provinces, IRSA, WAPDA, municipal governments*) is a content-empty pass — push for an actor even in grammar class.
