# Session 10 — Active and Passive Voice

**Module B · Time, voice, narration**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** voice conversion (older papers); essay style — agency vs anonymity

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Convert a sentence from active to passive and back **without changing tense**.
2. Form passive with modals (*must be taxed*), perfect (*has been taxed*), and continuous (*is being taxed*).
3. Choose voice **on purpose**: active for argument and blame; passive for process, law, or unknown actor.
4. Avoid empty passives (*steps should be taken*) and illegal passives of intransitive verbs.
5. Rewrite a bureaucratic paragraph into adult CSS English.

---

| Time | Move |

**Traps:** changing tense while converting; *by* stuffed onto every passive; passive of *happen, occur, die, consist, resemble*; *It is being rained*.

**Stretch:** double-object passives: *The poor were denied a hearing* / *A hearing was denied to the poor.*

## Student notes

### 1. Voice is about who is in the spotlight

- **Active:** the **doer** is the subject. *Parliament passed the bill.*  
- **Passive:** the **receiver** is the subject. *The bill was passed (by Parliament).*

Both are grammatical. CSS essays that hide every doer sound like a timid press release. CSS essays that never use passive cannot describe a process. You need **both**, on purpose.

### 2. The conversion formula

Active: **Doer + verb + object**  
Passive: **Object + *be* (same tense) + past participle + (by + doer)**

The object of the active sentence **must exist**. No object → no ordinary passive.

- *The monsoon arrived.* (intransitive) → no *The monsoon was arrived.*  
- *The crop failed.* → not *The crop was failed* (unless someone failed it).

### 3. *be* carries the tense

| Tense | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present simple | *The FBR taxes retailers.* | *Retailers **are taxed** (by the FBR).* |
| Present continuous | *The FBR is taxing retailers.* | *Retailers **are being taxed**.* |
| Present perfect | *The FBR has taxed retailers.* | *Retailers **have been taxed**.* |
| Past simple | *The FBR taxed retailers.* | *Retailers **were taxed**.* |
| Past continuous | *The FBR was taxing retailers.* | *Retailers **were being taxed**.* |
| Past perfect | *The FBR had taxed retailers.* | *Retailers **had been taxed**.* |
| Future | *The FBR will tax retailers.* | *Retailers **will be taxed**.* |
| Modal | *The FBR must tax retailers.* | *Retailers **must be taxed**.* |
| *going to* | *The FBR is going to tax retailers.* | *Retailers **are going to be taxed**.* |

There is normally **no** present/past perfect continuous passive in student writing (*has been being taxed*). Recast.

### 4. When CSS **should** use passive

1. Actor unknown: *The file was leaked.*  
2. Actor obvious or irrelevant: *The accused was sentenced to two years.*  
3. Process / science / law: *The bill was referred to committee.*  
4. You want to emphasise the victim or the object: *The witnesses were intimidated.*

### 5. When CSS **should** use active

1. Argument and blame: *The cabinet delayed the census.*  
2. Recommendations: *The Election Commission should publish the rolls.*  
3. Clarity: if the passive needs a long *by*-phrase, flip it.

**Empty passive (ban):** *Concrete steps should be taken. It is hoped that things will improve. It is to be noted that…*

**Named active (prefer):** *The Ministry of Finance should publish the true circular debt this quarter.*

### 6. Verbs that resist ordinary passive

*happen, occur, take place, appear, seem, belong, consist, resemble, lack, die, fall* (the weather), *rise*.

- *An accident **happened**.* not *was happened*  
- *The meeting **took place**.* not *was took place*  
- *He **died** in 2020.* not *was died*

*agree*: *The committee **agreed**.* / *It **was agreed** that…* (impersonal, legal). Not *The committee was agreed* unless *agreed* is an adjective: *They were agreed on a draft* — possible, but *had agreed* is safer.

### 7. *get*-passive

*got arrested, got promoted* — spoken. CSS: *was arrested, was promoted*.

### 8. By-agent: often drop it

If the doer is *someone, people, the authorities* — omit.  
If the doer is the point of the sentence — keep, or switch to active.

- Weak: *The law was passed by the Parliament of Pakistan in the year 2010.*  
- Stronger: *Parliament passed the law in 2010.*

## Urdu-interference box

Urdu *hua / gayi / diya gaya* tempts students to paste *was/were* onto every verb.

- *Accident ho gaya* → *An accident happened.* not *was happened*  
- *Wo CSS mein fail ho gaya* → *He failed CSS.* not *He was failed in CSS.*  
- *Faisla kiya gaya* → *A decision was taken* is legal but empty. *The cabinet decided…*

## Class drill

**A. Convert to passive. Keep the tense. Drop *by* if the doer is obvious.**

1. The Supreme Court struck down the ordinance.  
2. The ministry is drafting a new framework.  
3. The provinces have not implemented the order.  
4. WAPDA must meter the tube wells.  
5. Someone leaked the paper.

**B. Convert to active. Invent a precise doer if needed.**

6. It is hoped that inflation will fall.  
7. Concrete steps should be taken to improve education.  
8. The census was delayed.  
9. A decision was taken to subsidise electricity.

**C. Repair**

10. The accident was happened on the motorway.  
11. She was died of dengue.  
12. The bill is being pass tomorrow.  
13. The poor is ignored by policy.  
14. The meeting was taken place at dawn.

## Essay-transfer task

Question: **Who should be taxed more fairly — retailers, real estate, or the salaried class? (You may pick one line.)**

Write **two** 90–100 word paragraphs.

- **Paragraph A — active:** almost every sentence names a doer (FBR, Parliament, provincial boards, the salaried class).  
- **Paragraph B — passive:** describe the same policy as a process.

Then write **four lines** answering: Which paragraph is more honest? Where was passive actually better? Copy one sentence you would keep in a real CSS essay.

## Homework

1. Convert 10 active sentences of your own (education theme) into passive, then three of them back. Tick tense: it must not change.  
2. Rewrite this press-release into CSS English (mostly active, 80 words): *It is high time that steps should be taken by the authorities concerned so that the problem of urban flooding may be addressed and it is hoped that the masses will be facilitated in the near future.*  
3. Error log: every empty passive you find in your old essays.  
4. Preview: bring a short quoted sentence (a headline or a one-line report) for Session 11.

## Teacher mark pointers

- 5: *The paper was leaked.* (*by someone* is dead weight.)  
- 6: *Economists / the government / this essay hopes…* — force a doer. *I hope* is weak in CSS; *Inflation may fall if…* is better.  
- 13: *The poor **are** ignored* (adjective-as-noun = plural) even in passive.  
- Homework 2: a good recast names *city governments, drains, housing on floodplains*. Reward agency.
