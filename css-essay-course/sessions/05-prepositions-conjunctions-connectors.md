# Session 5 — Parts of Speech IV: Prepositions, Conjunctions, Connectors

**Module A · Foundation**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** FPSC preposition items (high-yield); essay cohesion; *although…but* errors

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Use high-frequency **verb + preposition** collocations that FPSC tests.
2. Distinguish *since/for, in/on/at, between/among, despite/in spite of*.
3. Join clauses with coordinators and subordinators without doubling (*although…but*).
4. Use a short list of **essay connectors** without stuffing every sentence.
5. Write a 6-sentence paragraph whose glue is grammar, not *moreover* in every line.

---

**Traps:** *discuss about, comprise of, despite of, according to me, between* for more than two, *on 2024*, *since five years*.

**Stretch:** *in contrast with* vs *in contrast to*; *compared with* (BrE preference) vs *compared to*.

## Student notes

### 1. A preposition is a relating word

It sits before a noun / pronoun / gerund and shows time, place, cause, means, or belonging.

- *in 2022, on Monday, at night*  
- *in Punjab, on the canal, at the secretariat*  
- *by law, through the courts, for the poor, of the state*

The object of a preposition is in **object form**: *between you and **me*** (not *I*).

### 2. Time and place (the skeleton)

| | **in** | **on** | **at** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Time | months, years, long periods: *in June, in 2018, in the 1990s* | days, dates, surfaces of time: *on Monday, on 14 August* | clock, meal, festival as a point: *at 5 p.m., at dawn, at Eid* (also *on Eid* in some BrE — pick *at Eid* or *on Eid day*) |
| Place | cities, countries, enclosed space: *in Karachi, in Pakistan, in the room* | surfaces, roads, floors: *on Mall Road, on the desk* | exact point: *at the airport, at the door, at 10 Constitution Avenue* |

- *arrive **in** Lahore* (city) / *arrive **at** the station*  
- *since 2018* (starting point) / *for six years* (period)  
- *by 2030* (deadline) / *until 2030* (up to that time)

### 3. Exam collocations (memorise)

| Correct | Not |
| --- | --- |
| discuss the bill | discuss **about** the bill |
| comprise 20 members | comprise **of** |
| consist **of** 20 members | consist 20 members |
| despite the rain / in spite **of** the rain | despite **of** |
| interested **in** | interested **on** |
| depend / rely **on** | depend **upon** is old-fashioned but not wrong; prefer *on* |
| accused **of**, charged **with** | accused with |
| good **at** maths, weak **in** grammar | good **in** maths (school English — avoid) |
| angry **with** a person, angry **at** a thing | angry **on** |
| die **of** disease, die **in** an accident | die from is also used for cause — accept *die of* in keys |
| married **to** | married **with** |
| different **from** | different **than** (AmE; avoid in CSS) |
| insist **on**, live **on** a wage | |
| responsible **for**, reason **for** | reason **of** (use *cause of* / *reason for*) |
| congratulated **on**, prevented **from** | |
| prefer X **to** Y, senior **to** | |
| according **to** the report | **according to me** → *in my view* |
| belong **to**, listen **to**, reply **to** | |

### 4. between vs among; beside vs besides

- *between* — two, or two-sided deals even if more parties: *between the federation and the provinces*  
- *among* — a group: *among the parties in the assembly*  
- *beside* = next to; *besides* = in addition to

### 5. Conjunctions: joining without doubling

**Coordinating (FANBOYS):** *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*  
They join **equals**. Comma before them when they join two full sentences.

**Subordinating:** *because, since, although, though, if, unless, until, when, while, after, before, as, whereas, so that*

A subordinator already does the job. Do **not** add a coordinator of the same meaning.

| Illegal double | Legal |
| --- | --- |
| Although the law exists, **but** it is dead. | Although the law exists, it is dead. / The law exists, **but** it is dead. |
| Because she was late, **so** she was marked absent. | Because she was late, she was marked absent. |
| If you work, **then only** you will pass. (Indian/Pakistani spoken) | If you work, you will pass. / Only if you work will you pass. |

**unless / until** (full drill in Session 12): no extra *not*.

*The bill will not pass **unless** the treasury is present.*  
Not: *unless the treasury is not present.*

### 6. Essay connectors — a short, adult list

Use **one or two per paragraph**, not one per sentence.

| Job | Use these | Avoid stacking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add | *also, in addition, besides* | *moreover, furthermore, what is more* in every line |
| Contrast | *however, yet, even so, in contrast, on the other hand* | *contrarily* |
| Cause | *because, since, therefore, consequently, as a result* | *henceforth* |
| Example | *for example, for instance, namely* | *as an illustration of this point* |
| Sequence | *first, then, finally* | *last but not the least* |
| Concession | *although, even though, granted that* | |
| Sum | *overall, in short, therefore* | *in a nutshell* (too chatty) |

**however** is not a conjunction. It cannot join two sentences with commas alone.

Wrong: *The law exists, however it is dead.*  
Right: *The law exists. However, it is dead.* / *The law exists; however, it is dead.*

## Urdu-interference box

Urdu *lekin, magar, kyunke, aur* map badly onto English if you keep both halves.

- *Although… but…* comes from *agarche… lekin…*  
- *discuss about* comes from *baat karna about* / *discuss karna*  
- *according to me* from *mere mutabiq* → write *in my view* or, better in CSS, drop the self and make the claim: *The evidence suggests…*

## Class drill

**A. Insert the preposition** (or Ø if none)

1. The panel discussed ___ the draft for three hours.  
2. The cabinet consists ___ twenty ministers.  
3. The cabinet comprises ___ twenty ministers.  
4. ___ 2010 the floods displaced millions.  
5. Pakistan has faced circular debt ___ 2010.  
6. She is interested ___ water law.  
7. He was accused ___ contempt.  
8. He was charged ___ contempt.  
9. Despite ___ the stay order, demolition began.  
10. The officer is senior ___ me.

**B. Repair the join**

11. Although inflation slowed, but food prices did not.  
12. Because the data were late, so the budget was blind.  
13. The policy is sound, however it is unfunded.  
14. Wait until the House does not rise. (intended: wait until the House rises)  
15. Between the four provinces the water fight continues.

## Essay-transfer task

Topic: **Social media is a tool, not a policy.**

Write **six sentences** that form one paragraph.

Rules:

- Use **exactly three** connectors, chosen from: *however, because, therefore, for example, although, in contrast, as a result*.  
- Use at least two of these collocations: *depend on, consist of, discuss, despite, interested in*.  
- No *moreover, furthermore, last but not the least, according to me*.

Then rewrite the same paragraph **without** any connector words, using only clause-joining grammar (*although, because, if*, relative clauses). Compare: which is cleaner?

---

## Homework

1. Memorise the collocation table. Cover the right column and test yourself.  
2. Write 10 original CSS sentences, each locking one collocation.  
3. 120 words on *Federalism in Pakistan depends on water and money*. Use *since/for* once each, *despite* once, *although* once (no *but*).  
4. Find and copy 5 preposition errors from your own previous writing into the error log.

---

## Exit ticket

1. *comprise* or *consist* — which takes *of*?  
2. Correct: *discuss about climate*.  
3. Why is *The law exists, however it is dead* wrong?  
4. *since* or *for*: ___ six years; ___ 2018.

## Teacher mark pointers

- A1: Ø (*discuss the draft*).  
- A3: Ø (*comprises twenty*). Some modern usage allows *comprised of*; FPSC keys still prefer no *of*. Teach the exam form.  
- A4: *In 2010*.  
- A5: *since 2010*.  
- A9: *Despite the stay order* (no *of*).  
- 14 is a logic trap: *until* + positive verb.  
- Reward the transfer paragraph that uses connectors as **hinges**, not as decoration.
