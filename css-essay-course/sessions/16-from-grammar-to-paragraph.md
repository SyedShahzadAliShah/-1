# Session 16 — From Grammar to Paragraph

**Module C · Accuracy and transfer**  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Exam link:** essay body paragraphs — cohesion, topic sentence, examiner demand for logical organisation

---

## Outcomes

The student can:

1. Write a **topic sentence** that is a claim, not a heading.
2. Build a **PEEL-style** paragraph (Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link) with legal tense movement.
3. Use **old-to-new** cohesion (repeat a key noun or a precise *this + noun*).
4. Stay in academic register without cliché or autobiography.
5. Produce one 140–160 word CSS paragraph with fewer than three grammar faults.

**Traps:** paragraph that is a list of slogans; *this* with no noun; five connectors; no evidence beat.

**Stretch:** a counter-sentence (*however*) in the middle without killing the topic sentence.

## Student notes

### 1. The paragraph is the essay in miniature

FPSC subject experts asked for:

- a compact topic sentence that shows you understood the topic  
- logical organisation  
- coherence between paragraphs  
- cohesion inside the argument  
- clarity  
- a finish

Session 16 trains **one paragraph**. Session 17 stacks four of them under a clock.

### 2. A topic sentence is a claim

| Heading (fail) | Claim (pass) |
| --- | --- |
| *Rule of law* | *The rule of law is the habit of applying the same rule to friends and opponents.* |
| *Problems of education* | *Public education fails first as a teacher problem, not as a building problem.* |
| *Climate change in Pakistan* | *Pakistan’s climate risk is a water-management failure before it is a carbon story.* |

A topic sentence is usually **present simple**, 12–22 words, no quotation, no *in this paragraph I will discuss*.

### 3. PEEL (adapted for CSS)

| Beat | Job | Typical tense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P** Point | topic sentence = claim | present simple |
| **E** Evidence | fact, example, named institution, short dated event | past simple or present perfect |
| **E** Explanation | why the evidence supports the claim | present; *this + noun* |
| **L** Link | back to the essay thesis or onward to the next idea | present; *therefore / yet / if* |

You may insert **However + limit** between Evidence and Explanation. One concession makes you look adult. Three concessions make you look lost.

### 4. Cohesion: glue, not decoration

Glue is usually **grammar**, not a connector word.

1. Repeat a key noun: *census … the census … that count*  
2. Use *this / that / these / such* **+ noun**: *this delay, such guesswork*  
3. Use a pronoun only when the antecedent is obvious  
4. Use a subordinator: *because, although, if, which*  
5. Then, if still needed, one sentence adverb: *however, therefore*

**Old-to-new:** end sentence 1 with an idea; start sentence 2 with that idea.

- *The census is late. **This delay** poisons the NFC.*  
  Not: *The census is late. Moreover, inflation is also a problem.* (new rabbit)

### 5. Register

Write as a future officer, not as a TV tickers, not as a Victorian.

- Prefer short verbs: *cut, fund, publish, meter, audit, tax, enforce.*  
- Prefer concrete nouns: *rolls, drains, canal, courtroom, classroom.*  
- Do not address the examiner.  
- Do not open with *I*. A later *this essay argues* once is enough; even that can go.

### 6. Model paragraph (study the verbs)

Topic: *A census is a political act.*

> A census is a political act because it decides who is counted as a claimant on the federal purse. Pakistan has postponed that count more than once, and each postponement has been explained as a technical hitch. In 2017 the exercise still left provinces arguing about the method as much as the number. This quarrel was not a statistician’s hobby: NFC shares, seats and development funds move with the table. If the next census is again treated as a tactic rather than as a public good, the federation will keep bargaining in the dark. A state that fears its own headcount cannot claim adult budgeting.

Check:

- Topic claim in present.  
- Present perfect for the repeating postponement.  
- One dated past.  
- *This quarrel* = cohesion.  
- Type-1 *if* for the future.  
- No *need of the hour*.  
- Length: about 130 words.

### 7. Failure modes

- **Slogan stack:** five present-simple moral sentences, no evidence.  
- **Tense soup:** past and present in the same beat without a reason.  
- **Connector addiction:** *moreover* four times.  
- **Ghost paragraph:** all passives, no institution named.  
- **Fragment:** *Which is a serious problem.*  
- **Splice:** *The census is late, the NFC is toxic.*

## Urdu-interference box

Urdu *mazmoon* paragraphs sometimes restart the topic every sentence (*Taleem bohat zaroori hai. Taleem ke baghair qaum nahi chal sakti. Taleem par budget kam hai.*). In English that reads like a hammer. Use cohesion: *Public education is the cheapest state-building tool. **That cheapness** is lost when teachers are absent from the payroll’s useful end: the classroom.*

## Essay-transfer task

Write **150 words** (±10) on:

**The rule of law is a daily habit of institutions, not a slogan.**

Constraints:

- PEEL beats (you may label them lightly in the margin).  
- One dated or institutional piece of evidence (court, parliament, police, FBR, ECP — keep it modest).  
- One *this + noun*.  
- One *although* or *if*.  
- Zero items from the Session 15 cliché list.  
- Circle every finite verb.

Partner marks with T/A/P/S only. Writer repairs in a different colour.

## Teacher mark pointers

- Award the transfer on **language first** (the 3-faults-per-100-words standard from the syllabus). Then a 5-mark structure tick: claim / evidence / link.  
- Do not require original research. Punish fake precision (*exactly 43.2%*) unless the student truly knows a figure. Modest evidence beats invented decimals.  
- If the partner used codes lazily (*S* on every line), make them re-mark one sentence with a named family.
