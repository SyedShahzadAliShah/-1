# CSS Essay Writing — Grammar Foundation Syllabus

**Course title:** From Sentence to Essay: Grammar for CSS Aspirants  
**Duration:** 17 sessions (90 minutes each)  
**Exam target:** CSS Compulsory English Essay (100) and language accuracy for Precis & Composition (100)  
**Language standard:** British English  
**Users:** Classroom teacher + CSS student

---

## 1. Why this course exists

FPSC’s own essay instruction is short:

> Candidates will be required to write one or more Essay in English. A wide choice of topics will be given. Candidates are expected to reflect comprehensive and research based knowledge on a selected topic. Candidate’s articulation, expression and technical treatment of the style of Essay writing will be examined.

Examiner reports keep repeating the same failures:

- incorrect and flawed language
- broken sentence structure
- tense mixing
- weak vocabulary or showy vocabulary used wrongly
- no logical organisation
- no cohesion between paragraphs
- Urdu thought translated word for word

A CSS essay is failed by **grammar before it is failed by ideas**. This syllabus therefore spends 17 sessions on the fundamentals the teacher already uses in class — parts of speech, tenses, voice, narration, and error removal — and forces every rule into essay writing.

---

## 2. Course aims

By the end of Session 17, the student will be able to:

1. Identify every word in a sentence by its **part of speech** and repair the sentence from that diagnosis.
2. Control the **12 tense forms** and keep one time-frame inside a paragraph.
3. Write **active and passive** sentences on purpose, not by accident.
4. Convert **direct speech to narration** when quoting reports, leaders, or thinkers.
5. Spot and correct the error families FPSC recycles: agreement, articles, prepositions, tense, conditionals, parallelism, modifiers, diction.
6. Remove **Pakistani/Urdu interference** (discuss about, I am agree, unless you don’t, one of the reason).
7. Build a **grammar-clean paragraph** with a topic sentence, evidence, and a link.
8. Produce a **timed mini-essay** (250–300 words) that would not be dismissed for language.

## 3. Design principles (for the teacher)

| Principle | Classroom meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Rule → Example → Essay transfer | Never teach a rule without a CSS-topic sentence |
| Diagnose, don’t decorate | Students name the error before they rewrite |
| Smallest correct change | Same FPSC instruction as sentence correction |
| British usage | *organisation, programme, towards, practise (v)* |
| Urdu contrast | Every session has a “don’t translate this” box |
| Two audiences | Left side of each session is teacher timing; the notes are student-ready |
| Recycle errors | Old mistakes return in later drills |

## 5. Session list (the 17 classes)

### Module A — Foundation

| No. | Title | Core grammar | CSS essay use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The CSS sentence and the examiner’s demand | Sentence vs fragment vs run-on; subject + predicate; four sentence types | Why language marks kill an otherwise informed essay |
| 2 | Parts of speech I — nouns, pronouns, articles | Countable/uncountable; one of the + plural; a/an/the/zero | *the state, governance, information, a policy* |
| 3 | Parts of speech II — verbs, auxiliaries, modals | Finite verbs; do/be/have; can/could/may/must/should/would | Hedging and force in argument (*must, should, would*) |
| 4 | Parts of speech III — adjectives, adverbs, determiners | Order of adjectives; comparison; much/many/few/little | Precise evaluation without empty intensifiers |
| 5 | Parts of speech IV — prepositions, conjunctions, connectors | in/on/at/of/to; although/but; cohesion devices | Paragraph glue: *however, therefore, in contrast* |

### Module B — Time, voice, narration

| No. | Title | Core grammar | CSS essay use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | Tenses I — the present system | Simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous | Universal claims, current affairs, ongoing crises |
| 7 | Tenses II — the past system | Simple, continuous, perfect, used to | History, case studies, “Pakistan in 1971 / 1998 / 2010” |
| 8 | Tenses III — future, mixed tenses, sequence | will/going to/present for future; sequence of tenses | Policy recommendations and consistent paragraphs |
| 9 | Agreement and sentence architecture | SVA; neither/nor; many a; collective nouns | Long CSS sentences that still agree |
| 10 | Active and passive voice | Form, by-agent, when passive is better | *The bill was passed* vs *Parliament passed the bill* |
| 11 | Narration — direct and indirect speech | Backshift; questions; commands; reporting verbs | Quoting a report, a leader, or a thinker without breaking tense |

### Module C — Accuracy and transfer

| No. | Title | Core grammar | CSS essay use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 | Conditionals and hypothetical policy | 0, 1, 2, 3, mixed; unless/until; wish/as if | “If Pakistan invested in…” arguments |
| 13 | Punctuation and sentence polish | comma, semicolon, colon, dash, apostrophe, quotation | Examiner-safe punctuation; no comma splices |
| 14 | Removing errors I | FPSC sentence-correction families | Exam-style rewrite with smallest change |
| 15 | Removing errors II | Parallelism, modifiers, diction, Urduisms | Cleaning a dirty CSS paragraph |
| 16 | From grammar to paragraph | Topic sentence, cohesion, academic register | One full argumentative paragraph |
| 17 | Capstone: timed mini-essay and remediation | All previous rules under 40 minutes | Diagnostic map for the rest of CSS prep |

## 6. Weekly rhythm (if you teach two or three times a week)

**Two sessions a week (9 weeks):**  
Mon/Thu or Tue/Fri. Diagnostic in Session 1. Mid-test after Session 9. Final in Session 17.

**Three sessions a week (6 weeks):**  
Same order. Do not skip Sessions 14–15; error hunting is where marks are recovered.

**One session a week (17 weeks):**  
Possible, but homework must be marked every week or the grammar will not stick.

## 7. Assessment

| Instrument | When | Marks | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnostic (30 items + 80-word paragraph) | Start of Session 1 | Unmarked or 50 | Placement; teacher sees Urdu-medium vs English-medium gaps |
| Exit ticket | End of every session | 4–6 items | Did today land? |
| Homework | After every session | 10–15 items + 80–120 words | Spaced practice |
| Mid-course test | After Session 9, or first 20 min of Session 10 | 50 | Tenses + agreement + parts of speech |
| Final timed mini-essay | Session 17 | 25 language + 15 structure | Transfer to essay conditions |
| Error log | Running | — | Student records every personal mistake; teacher samples logs in Sessions 9, 14, 17 |

## 8. Standard session shape (90 minutes)

| Minutes | Block | Teacher does | Student does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Recap / homework spot-check | 3 sentences on the board from last homework | Correct in notebooks |
| 8–28 | Teach the rule | Board + contrastive Urdu box | Copy the formula sheet lines only |
| 28–48 | Guided drill | Cold-call, then pair check | Diagnose → rewrite |
| 48–72 | Essay transfer | One CSS topic, 6–8 sentences or a paragraph | Write, then swap |
| 72–85 | Error hunt from student writing | Public correction of 4 real sentences (anonymous) | Copy corrected versions |
| 85–90 | Homework + exit ticket | Assign; collect tickets | 4 quick items |

## 11. Pass standard (teacher’s professional judgement)

A student is **language-ready for full CSS essay practice** when all of these are true:

- Fewer than **3 grammar faults** per 100 words in a timed paragraph
- No tense drift inside a paragraph
- Articles and prepositions at least 80% accurate
- Can convert a quotation to indirect speech
- Can write 8–10 sentences in mixed active/passive on purpose
- Can name the error in a faulty sentence before correcting it

Until then, do not send the student into 2500-word essay drills. Length without grammar produces a longer fail.

---

## 12. Recommended sequence after this course

1. Full CSS essay structure (outline, thesis, 8–10 paragraphs, conclusion)
2. Precis, comprehension, pair of words, translation
3. Content banks: governance, climate, gender, foreign policy, economy, technology
4. Weekly timed 2500–3000 word essay under exam conditions

This 17-session pack is the foundation. Do not invert the order.
