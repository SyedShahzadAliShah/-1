# Teacher Handbook

Use this before Session 1 and as a weekly briefing. Session files contain the lesson; this file contains the **method**.

---

## 1. The student in front of you

CSS batches in Pakistan are mixed:

| Profile | Typical strength | Typical wound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English-medium, big-city | Reading fluency, some vocabulary | Bombast, run-ons, fake “CSS style” |
| Urdu-medium, solid content | Ideas, current affairs memory | Articles, prepositions, tense, word order |
| Repeaters | Familiarity with topics | Fossilised errors (“I am agree”) |
| High-vocabulary crammers | Rare words | Wrong collocation, wrong register |

**Rule:** teach to the wound, not the ego. A student who can recite 40 quotations still fails if the verb does not agree.

---

## 2. What FPSC is actually marking in language

From examiner feedback on the Essay paper, language is not a side issue. The subject experts ask for:

- correct and flawless language
- appropriate vocabulary (not ornamental)
- clarity
- coherence between paragraphs
- cohesion inside the argument
- a proper beginning with a topic sentence
- an impressive finish

Your job in 17 sessions is **flawless language + cohesion**. Content and “research-based knowledge” are a later course. If you mix too much current affairs into grammar class, the grammar will not be learned.

---

## 3. Non-negotiable classroom rules

1. **English in the room** after the first 5 minutes of Session 1. Urdu is allowed only in the labelled “Urdu box” moments (30–60 seconds to contrast a structure).
2. **No rewriting by taste.** Students must name the rule: *agreement / article / tense / preposition / voice / narration / punctuation / diction*.
3. **Smallest correct change** — same instruction as CSS sentence correction. Do not let students recast a whole sentence to hide the error.
4. **Anonymous public correction.** Take 4 student sentences each class. Do not name the writer. Shame kills batches; patterns must still be shown.
5. **Error log is compulsory.** If there is no log, the homework is incomplete.
6. **British spelling.** Correct *organization → organisation* unless a proper noun.
7. **Ban the fake CSS sentence.** *“In this contemporary epoch of globalization, it is crystal clear that…”* is a fail style. Prefer short, exact English.

---

## 4. How to mark homework in 20 minutes (even with 40 scripts)

Do **not** line-edit every word. Use a four-code system. Write the code in the margin; the student repairs.

| Code | Meaning | Student action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **T** | Tense / time | Fix the verb form and time marker |
| **A** | Article / agreement | a/an/the/zero or SVA |
| **P** | Preposition / pair / punctuation | Replace the small word or mark |
| **S** | Structure (fragment, run-on, voice, modifier) | Rebuild the sentence |

Pick **one gold sentence** and **one wrecked sentence** per script. Star the gold. Bracket the wreck. In the next recap, put two wrecks on the board.

If you try to correct everything, you will drown and the student will copy your English instead of learning the rule.

---

## 5. How to handle mixed ability

- **Weaker students:** they must master Sessions 2, 5, 6, 9, 14. That is articles, prepositions, present tense, agreement, error hunting.
- **Stronger students:** same drill, then an extra “stretch” sentence in each session file. Do not let them skip the drill; their errors are usually hidden in long sentences.
- **Pairing:** always pair a fluent speaker with a content-strong Urdu-medium student for the essay-transfer block. The fluent student may not be the grammatically accurate one. Check.

---

## 6. Speaking vs writing

CSS is a written exam. Do not spend the hour on oral fluency games. A short oral recitation of a formula is useful (30 seconds). The marks come from the page. Every session ends with writing.

---

## 7. The “CSS topic” bank for transfer tasks

Stay inside these themes so grammar class also feels like CSS class, without becoming a current-affairs lecture:

1. Governance and the rule of law  
2. Education and the youth bulge  
3. Climate and water  
4. Economy, inflation, unemployment  
5. Gender and social justice  
6. Extremism and social cohesion  
7. Technology, AI, social media  
8. Foreign policy and regional peace  
9. Population and urbanisation  
10. Democracy, civil-military balance (handle with academic tone only)

One theme per transfer task is enough. The student is practising **sentences**, not producing a full essay until Session 17.

---

## 8. Banned student habits (put this on the wall)

- Starting every essay with a dictionary definition  
- *According to me*  
- *Last but not the least*  
- *I am agree / I am not agree*  
- *Discuss about / comprise of / despite of / return back*  
- *The need of the hour is that government should take concrete steps* (empty)
- Three clauses joined by commas  
- Changing tense every sentence  
- Quoting without a reporting frame  
- Using *thou, henceforth, wherein* to look classical  

---

## 9. What success looks like by session cluster

**After Session 5:** student can label parts of speech in a 25-word sentence and repair articles/prepositions.

**After Session 9:** a 120-word paragraph stays in one tense and agrees throughout.

**After Session 13:** student can punctuate and convert voice/narration.

**After Session 17:** a 250–300 word mini-essay on a known CSS theme, timed, with a thesis, three body sentences-groups, and a conclusion — language not collapsing.

---

## 10. If you only have 12 sessions

Cut in this order (painful but honest):

- Fold Session 4 into 2–3 (adjectives/adverbs as a 15-minute insert)
- Fold Session 12 into 8 (conditionals as future-tense extension)
- Keep 14 and 15. Error removal is the exam skill.
- Never cut 6, 9, 16, 17.

If you have extra time, repeat 14–15 with new sentences. That is the highest-yield revision.

---

## 11. Ethics and tone

CSS essays on gender, religion, and civil-military relations require **academic calm**. Model that tone in example sentences. Do not use partisan slogans as grammar examples. Do not mock a student’s accent. Correct the sentence.

---

## 12. Before you walk in (2-minute checklist)

- [ ] Session file open; answers not on the student printout  
- [ ] Three recap sentences from last week’s homework  
- [ ] Board plan: formula on the left, examples on the right  
- [ ] Transfer topic written at the top before class starts  
- [ ] Exit tickets cut  
- [ ] Error-log reminder  
- [ ] Phone away; students write on paper, not only on screens
